Price your round

Take the funding ask from Exercise 5 and price it two ways: the share an investor would get for your money, and the share they would need to earn their return. Then close the gap, negotiate, and decide what you would sign.

Is there a real investor?

Use the total from Exercise 5. Most groups work with a practice offer. That’s fine: the point is to see what your forecast has to support.

2 The test offer

An angel offers to put in your ask in exchange for part of the company. They name a pre-money valuation: what they think the company is worth before their money goes in.

Early rounds commonly sell 10–25% of a company, so try a valuation of 3 to 9 times your ask.

Your turn: work out the deal

Post-money valuation is what the company is worth right after the money goes in. The investor owns their money’s share of it.

3 What the investor needs

Investors work backward from the day they hope to sell their shares. They ask what the company could sell for, then what that is worth today given the return they need. This is the venture capital method.

Annual revenue, not the total over all years. This is your group’s number to defend.

A revenue multiple is what buyers pay for each dollar of annual revenue. Early-stage investors aim for 50–60% a year because most of their bets fail.
